## 0. Market read — what "premium" actually looks like here

Observed from the top of this market (Big Life Journal, Lovevery, Highlights, Panda Planner, TPT "Gold" sellers, KDP kids' bestsellers):

**What reads PREMIUM**
1. **Warm paper ground, not white.** Lovevery's identity sits on a warm cream field (~`#F7F4ED` family) rather than `#FFFFFF`. Cream reads as "book"; pure white reads as "worksheet printed at the office."
2. **Restraint in hue count.** Premium kid brands run 3–5 colors with an obvious hierarchy. Cheap printables run 9+ colors of equal loudness.
3. **Desaturated + one pop.** The palette is muted; exactly one saturated color does the shouting. Fast-food garish = three or more fully saturated colors fighting.
4. **Illustration is one system, one hand.** Every mark looks like it came from the same pen. Big Life Journal's equity is a consistent illustrated world, not the individual drawings.
5. **Typographic hierarchy does the work.** Pentagram's Lovevery system leans on strong type and a color-tab wayfinding motif, on minimal brown packaging — i.e. the *structure* signals quality, not decoration.
6. **Generous white space.** The dominant "amateur" tell in printables is edge-to-edge density; white space around text improves readability, and around illustration creates focus.
7. **Material honesty.** High-resolution vector output, correct proportions, aligned elements, nothing stretched.

**What reads CLIPART CHEAP**
- Stock clip art with mismatched line weights and outline styles on one page.
- Drop shadows with blur, gradient meshes, gloss highlights, bevels.
- Rainbow "Comic Sans energy" — every heading a different color.
- Distorted (non-proportionally scaled) art or type.
- WordArt-style outlined/arched titles.
- Photographic food cut-outs pasted next to flat vector art.
- Decorative elements with no job (random stars, swooshes, sparkle bursts).
- Full-bleed color fills that a home inkjet will band, streak, and warp the paper with.

**The single design decision that carries this brand:** a warm cream ground + a two-hue system + one saturated pop + a single illustrated hand + white space that is *obviously* deliberate.

### 1.2 The winner — **FLIP THE BOX**

**Lockup:** `FLIP THE BOX` — "FLIP" set large, "THE BOX" set at 38% of cap height beneath it, flush left.
**Master tagline:** *The front of the box is an ad. The back of the box is the facts.*
**Short promise line (retail/sales page):** *Read any food label in ten seconds. Together.*

**Why it wins:**

1. **It names a behavior, not a verdict.** Every competitor name in this category encodes a judgment ("detectives" hunt wrongdoing, "decoders" expose deception, "patrols" police). "Flip" is a physical action with zero moral content. This is the *only* lane that survives Hard Rule #2 (no food moralizing) at the name level, and it is the reason this name is worth more than the cleverer ones above.
2. **It is the video hook.** The owner's motto is *Entertain the Masses. Educate the Interested. Sell to the Committed.* An imperative, demonstrable, one-gesture name means every TikTok opens with the product's own name as the action: hand picks up a box, flips it, camera pushes in. The brand and the hook are the same object.
3. **It is teachable and repeatable at home.** "We flip first" becomes a family rule. Big Life Journal's real product is a ritual, not a book. Same mechanic here.
4. **It carries the thesis.** *Front = ad. Back = facts.* This reframes the whole product from nutrition advice (contested, risky, medical-adjacent) to **media literacy** (uncontested, defensible, backlash-proof). That single move is the brand's strategic moat.
5. **It works at 200×300px.** "FLIP" is four letters. Set at 22% of cover width it is legible in a Kindle carousel and on a phone at arm's length. "Nutrition Facts Label Detectives" is not.
6. **It scales into an architecture** (§1.4) without renaming anything.

## 2. VOICE

Two voices, one brain. They never blend on the same page. The parent voice never appears in a kid book; the kid voice never appears in the parent guide. A kid reading over a parent's shoulder should find the parent book *boring*, not scary.

### 2.1 Parent voice — calm, authoritative, never preachy, never fear-based

The reader is a competent adult who is tired and slightly defensive. Assume they have already been lectured today. Our job is to hand them a tool and get out of the way.

| # | DO | DON'T |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "You already know how your kid eats. This gives you a way to talk about it that doesn't start a fight." | "Most parents have no idea what they're feeding their children." |
| 2 | "The panel is a spec sheet. That's all it is. Here's how to read a spec sheet." | "Food companies are hiding the truth from your family." |
| 3 | "Serving size is the first line for a reason — every other number on the panel depends on it." | "Serving size is the #1 trick they use to fool you!" |
| 4 | "If your kid asks for it, buy it and read it together. Reading it is the lesson." | "Keep these items out of the house." |
| 5 | "You don't need to memorize anything. You need to know where to look, in what order." | "Every parent should know these 27 ingredients by heart." |
| 6 | "Two kids in the same family will land in different places on this. That's fine." | "The right answer is always the one with fewer ingredients." |
| 7 | "This is a reading skill, not a diet. Nobody in this book is on a diet." | "Small changes now will prevent big problems later." |
| 8 | "We cite the source for every number in this book. If we couldn't source it, we left it out." | "Studies show…" (with no citation) |
| 9 | "If a label makes you anxious, put the box down and come back to it. The skill keeps." | "You should feel uncomfortable about what's in this." |
| 10 | "Here's the exact script to use in the cereal aisle when the answer is no." | "Be firm. Children need boundaries around food." |

**Parent-voice mechanics:** second person. 14–20 word average sentence. One idea per paragraph. Never more than two sentences before a concrete example. Bold is used for *actions*, never for alarm. Every claim that contains a number ends with a superscript source marker keyed to `01-FACTS/nutrition-fact-base.md`.

### 2.2 Kid voice — playful, fast, respects their intelligence, zero baby talk

The reader is a smart person who is short. Never explain the joke. Never use a word like "yummy." Let them be right about something on every spread.

| # | DO | DON'T |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Flip it. What's the very first line?" | "Let's flip our yummy snack box over, boys and girls!" |
| 2 | "That number is per serving. How many servings are in the whole bag? Check." | "Uh oh! Did you eat the WHOLE bag?? 😱" |
| 3 | "Blurb says it's 'made with real fruit.' Made with. Not made *of*. Big difference." | "Blurb is lying to you. Never trust the front!" |
| 4 | "Two boxes. Same food. Different numbers. Which one surprised you?" | "Which one is the healthy choice?" |
| 5 | "Ingredients are listed heaviest first. So whatever's #1, there's the most of that." | "The first ingredient is the most important one for your growing body!" |
| 6 | "You just read a chemical name out loud on the first try. That's the whole skill." | "Great job, superstar! ⭐⭐⭐" |
| 7 | "Nobody's taking your chips. We're just reading the bag." | "It's okay to have treats sometimes, in moderation." |
| 8 | "Pick any package in your kitchen. Any one. We'll wait." | "Ask a grown-up to help you find a healthy snack." |
| 9 | "Tally counts. Blurb talks. Guess which one you should copy." | "Tally is the good guy and Blurb is the bad guy!" |
| 10 | "Wrong is fine. Wrong and then checking is the entire job." | "Try again! You can do it!" |

**Kid-voice mechanics:** 6–9 tier — 8–12 words per sentence, one instruction per line, present tense, Andika set at 16pt. 10–13 tier — 12–16 words, dry humor allowed, sarcasm about *marketing* allowed, sarcasm about *people* never. Neither tier uses exclamation marks more than once per spread. Zero emoji in the printed books (emoji are a social-media-only asset).

### 2.3 Banned words and approved replacements

The rule behind every row: **name the number, not the food.** A food is never the subject of a value judgment; a nutrient amount is a fact you read.

| Banned | Why it's banned | Approved replacement |
|---|---|---|
| junk food | Categorizes foods as worthless; predicts disordered eating language | name the actual food ("chips," "cookies," "the cereal") |
| bad food / good food | The core moralizing frame this brand exists to replace | "higher in `{{NUTRIENT}}`" / "lower in `{{NUTRIENT}}`" |
| healthy / unhealthy (as a food label) | Unfalsifiable, context-free, and legally fraught | state the nutrient and the amount |
| clean / dirty | Purity language; direct orthorexia vocabulary | "short ingredient list" / "long ingredient list" |
| toxic / poison / chemicals | Fear-based and factually sloppy | "an ingredient you don't recognize — let's look it up" |
| cheat meal / cheat day | Frames eating as rule-breaking | "a food you love" |
| guilty / guilt-free / sinful / naughty | Attaches morality to appetite | delete the word; no replacement needed |
| treat / reward | Positions food as payment for behavior | "a food you eat for fun" |
| empty calories | Sounds scientific, means nothing | "calories without much `{{NUTRIENT}}` alongside" |
| superfood | Marketing term, not a category | "a food with a lot of `{{NUTRIENT}}`" |
| detox / cleanse / flush | Pseudoscience | delete; never replaced |
| fattening | Body-shaming by implication | "higher in calories" |
| processed / ultra-processed | Real scientific term, routinely used as a slur | "packaged food" in general copy. The term *ultra-processed* may appear **only** when directly citing `01-FACTS` with the definition attached. |
| skinny / chubby / overweight / obese / "baby weight" | We never describe a body, anyone's, ever | no replacement — bodies are not a topic in this product |
| burn it off / earn it / work it off | Links eating to exercise as punishment | no replacement — delete the sentence |
| should / must / never (about eating) | Prescriptive; also edges toward medical advice | "you can decide" / "here's what the number says" |
| hidden sugar / sneaky / they don't want you to know | Conspiracy register; also inaccurate (it's printed on the box) | "it's listed under a different name — here are the names" |
| everything in moderation | The lecture every parent has already heard | delete; replace with the reading skill |
| I'm being good today | Self-moralizing, modeled by adults | "I already ate, I'm not hungry" |

**Enforcement:** `09-QA/` runs a regex lint over all copy in `03-`, `04-`, `05-`, `06-`, `07-` against this list. Any hit blocks the build. The word list is exported as `09-QA/banned-words.txt` in the same order.

### 2.4 Three sentences that define the brand

1. *The front of the box is an ad. The back of the box is the facts.*
2. *We're not taking anything away from you. We're adding one skill.*
3. *Name the number, not the food.*

## 3. COLOR SYSTEM

### 3.1 Design constraints and how each is solved

| Constraint | Solution |
|---|---|
| Appetizing, not fast-food garish | Warm cream ground + one deep cool anchor + **exactly one** saturated warm pop (Paprika). No two fully saturated hues ever appear at equal area on a page. |
| Prints beautifully on a home inkjet | No color exceeds 3 inks in its naive CMYK build except Paprika and Blueberry; no page exceeds **18% average area coverage** in flood color; nothing sits within **0.25 in / 6.35 mm** of trim (home inkjet unprintable margin); no tint below 8% (inkjets band and dither below that). |
| Works in grayscale | Every functional color pair is separated by ≥ 20 L\* (see §3.6). The one grayscale collision in the palette is documented with a hard usage rule. |
| Passes WCAG AA | Every text/background pairing is measured, printed in §3.4, and no pairing ships below 4.5:1 for body or 3.0:1 for ≥ 18.66px bold / ≥ 24px regular. |

### 3.4 Semantic set — the **AMOUNT LADDER**

**The problem.** A high/low indicator on a nutrition label is the single most dangerous element in this product. Red-vs-green would (a) fail for the ~8% of boys with red-green color vision deficiency, (b) collapse in grayscale, and (c) — the real problem — teach exactly the good-food/bad-food frame the brand exists to destroy. Green fiber and red sodium is a moral verdict wearing a lab coat.

**The solution: one hue, three lightnesses, plus a mandatory glyph.**

Amount is encoded as **density of ink**, not as hue. More ink = more of the thing. That is a literal, physical metaphor a six-year-old understands instantly, and it is definitionally colorblind-safe because no hue discrimination is required — only lightness, which every form of CVD preserves.

| Token | Label | HEX | CMYK (naive) | L\* | Glyph | Text on it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `--dv-high` | **A LOT** | `#2B3F55` | 49 / 26 / 0 / 67 | 25.9 | `▲▲▲` | white `#FFFFFF` |
| `--dv-mid` | **SOME** | `#5F7588` | 30 / 14 / 0 / 47 | 48.2 | `▲▲` | white `#FFFFFF` only |
| `--dv-low` | **A LITTLE** | `#CDD9E2` | 9 / 4 / 0 / 11 | 86.1 | `▲` | Ink `#22201D` |

**The four rules that make this non-moralizing — all four are non-negotiable:**

1. **The same color is used for every nutrient.** A LOT of fiber and A LOT of sodium print in the identical `#2B3F55`. The chip reports quantity. It never reports approval.
2. **Color never travels alone.** Every chip carries its triangle glyph *and* its word. Remove the color entirely and the chip still works — that is the accessibility test and it must pass on every instance.
3. **The threshold values live in `01-FACTS`, not here.** The `{{DV_HIGH_PCT}}` / `{{DV_LOW_PCT}}` cut-offs are sourced and cited in `01-FACTS/nutrition-fact-base.md`; this document specifies only the visual encoding.
4. **Every appearance of the ladder is accompanied, at least once per spread, by the line:** *"A lot of something isn't good or bad. It depends what you're looking for."*

**Dark-mode inversion.** In dark mode the metaphor inverts to preserve meaning: more *light* = more of the thing. `--dv-high` becomes the brightest value (`#DCE8F5`) and `--dv-low` the dimmest (`#4A5566`). Density of signal, not of ink. This is handled in `tokens.css` and is the one token group whose light and dark values are not simple tonal swaps.

### 3.8 Print production notes

- **CMYK values above are naive device-independent conversions, not profile output.** Before any commercial print run, rebuild every swatch in the actual output profile (US Web Coated SWOP v2 for US offset; FOGRA39 for EU) and re-approve on a press proof. The naive values are for estimating ink load and for specifying spot equivalents, not for plating.
- **Home-inkjet rules (the primary delivery channel):**
  - The PDF ships in **sRGB, not CMYK.** Consumer drivers convert badly from CMYK; sRGB is what an inkjet driver expects.
  - `--color-bg` resolves to `#FFFFFF` in the `@media print` block. We never make a customer print a full page of cream — it costs them a cartridge and warps the sheet.
  - No fill below 8% tint and no fill above 60% tint on a page a customer prints. 100% Blueberry appears only as type and rules.
  - Page ink budget: **≤ 18% average area coverage.** `08-BUILD` computes this per page and fails the build above 22%.
  - Minimum rule weight 0.5pt (0.18mm). Below that, inkjets drop the line intermittently.
  - Minimum reversed (knockout) type: 9pt Bold. Smaller reversed type fills in on plain paper.
  - Nothing within 0.25in / 6.35mm of trim on any page. No bleed on home-print pages, ever.
- **Grayscale PDF is a separate ship artifact,** not a printer-driver conversion. `08-BUILD` renders `*-GRAYSCALE.pdf` from the same source with `--theme=mono`, in which all color resolves to the L\* ladder above.

### 4.2 Why each face

**Fraunces — display.** A variable old-style with a `SOFT` axis (softens terminals) and a `WONK` axis (swaps in intentionally odd, "wonky" glyph forms). At `SOFT 50, WONK 1` it reads warm and a little handmade without being a script or a "kid font." That is precisely the register premium kids' publishing lives in: a book, not a worksheet. Its optical-size axis means the same family covers a 96pt cover title and a 20pt chapter head without the cover looking thin or the head looking clubby. **Setting:** `opsz` tracks point size; `SOFT 40` for parent, `SOFT 60` for kids; `WONK 1` for covers and chapter openers only, `WONK 0` for running heads.

**Source Serif 4 — parent body text.** Built for extended on-screen and in-print reading, with a large x-height, open apertures and sturdy serifs that hold at 10.5pt on uncoated home-printer stock. A serif body in the parent book is a positioning decision as much as a reading one: it says *book*, which is what separates a $27 guide from a free PDF.

**Lexend — sans, UI, and the 10–13 tier.** Designed explicitly to reduce visual crowding and improve reading performance, with a wide range of variable width. It is unadorned enough that a 12-year-old will not feel condescended to, and its clarity makes it the right face for interface-like elements: chips, tabs, table headers, the Amount Ladder labels.

**Andika — the 6–9 tier.** SIL's purpose-built literacy face. Single-storey **a** and **g**, an unambiguous **I / l / 1**, and letterforms that match the shapes a first-grader is being taught to *write*. This is not a stylistic pick; using a two-storey `a` in a book for six-year-olds is a legibility error. Set at 16pt minimum.

**Atkinson Hyperlegible Next — the accessible edition.** Built by the Braille Institute for maximum character differentiation. **Honest framing, which the product must also use:** the evidence does not support dyslexia-specific typefaces outperforming a well-set conventional sans. What reliably helps is larger size, more leading, more letter-spacing, shorter measure, and off-white paper. So the "Accessible Edition" of every book ships a *composite* change, not just a font swap — see §4.5. **OpenDyslexic** (SIL OFL) is offered as a one-click alternate in `08-BUILD` for readers who prefer it, with no efficacy claim attached.

**Arimo — the panel replica.** The FDA's Nutrition Facts label is specified in Helvetica Black and Helvetica Regular. Arimo is metrically compatible with Arial/Helvetica and free for commercial use, so a panel we draw lines up with a panel the kid is holding, character for character. Using anything else makes our reproductions subtly wrong, and this product cannot afford subtly wrong.

## 5. LAYOUT SYSTEM

### 5.1 The dual-trim problem, and the Universal Live Block

This product sells internationally and is printed by the customer on whatever is in their tray. Two trims must be supported with **one** layout — re-laying-out every page twice is how quality dies.

Solution: design once into a **Universal Live Block** of **479 × 680 pt (6.65 × 9.44 in / 169 × 240 mm)**, which fits inside both trims with legal margins. The block position changes per trim; nothing inside it moves.

| | **US Letter** | **A4** |
|---|---|---|
| Trim | 8.5 × 11 in = 612 × 792 pt | 210 × 297 mm = 595.28 × 841.89 pt |
| Inside (binding) margin | **72 pt** (1.000 in) | **70.87 pt** (25 mm) |
| Outside margin | **61 pt** (0.847 in) | **45.35 pt** (16 mm) |
| Top margin | **54 pt** (0.750 in) | **66 pt** (23.3 mm) |
| Bottom margin | **58 pt** (0.806 in) | **95.9 pt** (33.8 mm) |
| Live block | **479 × 680 pt** | **479 × 680 pt** |
| Baselines available | **136** | 136 (A4 simply carries more bottom air) |

Rationale for the asymmetry: the bottom margin is always larger than the top (optical centering — a block centered mathematically looks low). A4 absorbs its extra 50pt of height entirely at the foot, so the *top* of every page aligns identically across trims. A reader printing the same book on Letter and A4 gets pages that stack.

### 5.2 Safe zones

| Zone | Keep-clear | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **US 3-hole punch** | 0 → 0.875 in (63 pt) from the binding edge | US standard: 1/4 in holes, centers 1/2 in from edge, 4-1/4 in apart, vertically centered. 0.5 + 0.125 radius + 0.25 safety = 0.875 in. Our 1.0 in inside margin **clears by 0.125 in.** |
| **ISO 838 four-hole (A4)** | 0 → 21 mm from the binding edge | 6 mm holes, centers 12 mm from edge, 80 mm apart. 12 + 3 + 6 safety = 21 mm. Our 25 mm inside margin **clears by 4 mm.** |
| **Coil / comb / disc binding** | 0 → 0.75 in (54 pt) | Punch pattern plus visual clearance. Cleared by the 1.0 in inside margin. |
| **Saddle-stitch (booklet fold)** | add 0.0625 in creep per 4-sheet signature, shifting outward | Only applies to the commercially-printed edition. `08-BUILD` applies creep automatically when `--bind=saddle`. |
| **Home-inkjet unprintable margin** | **0.25 in / 6.35 mm on all four edges, absolute** | Consumer inkjets cannot print to the edge; many cannot print within 0.17 in and most warp paper trying. **No bleed on home-print pages. Ever.** Our smallest margin (45.35 pt = 0.63 in) clears by 0.38 in. |
| **Perforation / tear-line (cards, fridge tools)** | 0.1875 in (13.5 pt) either side | Household scissors are not a trimmer. |
| **Lamination trim** | 0.125 in (9 pt) beyond trim on laminated fridge assets | Pouch laminators need a sealed border. |

### 5.3 Baseline grid

- **Unit: 5 pt.** 136 baselines per live block.
- Every leading value in §4.3 is a whole multiple of 5 pt. Every vertical space token (§5.5) is a whole multiple of 5 pt.
- **First baseline** sits at 15 pt below the top of the live block (so the first line's cap height optically aligns with the block top).
- Images, boxes and rules snap their **top and bottom edges** to the grid; the art inside them does not have to.
- The Nutrition Facts panel replica is the single sanctioned off-grid object — it is a fixed-geometry artifact reproduced at true size. It snaps its *bounding box* to the grid and floats internally.
- Screen equivalent: `--baseline: 0.5rem` (8px). All vertical rhythm on screen is a multiple of 8px.
