## F. THE OFFER LADDER

Working product name used below: **THE 10-SECOND READ** — chosen because Section A.2 shows the winners sell a *timed ritual*, not information. Final naming belongs to `02-BRAND/`.

### Tier 0 — "The Aisle Card" · **FREE**

| | |
|---|---|
| **What's inside** | One page, front and back. Front: the **10-Second Read** — the exact 3-step decision sequence at the shelf (servings → first three ingredients → the one %DV that matters today). Back: the **Serving Size Trap** (the single highest-aha concept) + a fill-in "our family's 3 questions" panel. Designed to be printed, folded once, and put in a wallet or on the fridge. Dyslexia-friendly version included |
| **Who it's for** | Persona 3, the TikTok health-conscious mom. Cold traffic |
| **Price rationale** | $0. Competing with free FDA content means the free tier must be *better than the FDA's*, not a teaser |
| **Psychological job** | **Prove competence and capture the email.** It must work on its own — a genuinely useful artifact someone keeps. Per Section E, the opt-in is worth ~10x the cold sale |
| **How it makes the paid product obvious** | Step 3 of the Scan is the only step a one-pager can't teach ("read the ingredients like a pro" needs practice reps). The card names that gap and points to the workbook. Also: the card is adult-facing — the natural next question is *"how do I get my kid to do this?"*, which is exactly Tier 1 |
| ⚠️ **Rule** | Never gate it behind more than an email. No phone number, no quiz funnel |

| Tier | Name | Price | Launch | Target buyer | Expected mix [ESTIMATE] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | The Aisle Card | **$0** | $0 | Cold TikTok traffic | ~25% of link clicks opt in |

**Blended AOV [ESTIMATE]:** (0.55 × $17) + (0.35 × $47) + (0.10 × $97) = **$35.55**. Launch-week AOV will run lower (~$28) on discounted pricing.

**⚠️ One deliberate omission: there is no subscription tier.** Solid Starts supports one because it has an app and an ongoing feeding problem to solve ([src](https://solidstarts.com/products/solid-starts-pro-membership/)). Label literacy is a skill you *acquire and then have* — there's no honest recurring value, and a subscription on a finished skill produces churn, refunds and bad reviews. Revisit only if you build a product with ongoing content.

## G. THE BONUS STACK

**Ground rule:** every stated value is anchored to something that actually sells at that price in Section A of the market research. No 10x fantasy math — a 4x value-to-price ratio that survives scrutiny beats a 12x one that insults the buyer. A homeschool mom who has priced curriculum *will* check.

| # | Bonus | Value | **Objection it kills** | Why the value is defensible |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Parent Script Cards** — word-for-word answers to the 12 hardest kid questions ("Is this bad for me?", "Why can't I have it?", "Is Emma's mom right?") | **$19** | *"I'll say the wrong thing and give my kid a complex."* The #1 unspoken fear, and the reason a "no food-shaming" product is bought instead of admired | Comparable scripted parenting printables sit in the Etsy $12–20 band; this is deeper and longer |
| 2 | **Bilingual Spanish/English Edition** — full translation, not machine output | **$47** | *"My mom/abuela watches the kids and doesn't read English."* Border-market reality, and a market-research §A.3 gap nobody fills | Equal to the core product price — it is a complete second edition, not an appendix |
| 3 | **Allergy & Carb-Counting Module** — allergen statements, "may contain," carb counting for bolusing | **$29** | *"Does it cover MY thing?"* — Persona 1's exact words. 5.8% of US kids have a food allergy ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db459.htm)); 12% of Americans have diabetes ([ADA](https://diabetes.org/about-diabetes/statistics/about-diabetes)) | Specialist medical-adjacent content; the narrower the audience the higher the per-unit value |
| 4 | **4-Week Scope & Sequence + Assessment Rubric** | **$24** | *"Is this a curriculum or a pile of worksheets?"* The homeschool buyer's gatekeeping question | A full TPT unit bundle with lesson structure sells at $3 — but a *4-week sequence with objectives and a rubric* is a different object; priced against the ~$89 site-license tier, $24 is conservative |
| 5 | **The Fridge Set** — 3 laminate-ready posters: the 10-Second Read, the Serving Size Trap, the Sugar Names Decoder | **$17** | *"This'll get used once and go in a drawer."* Wall-mounted = daily reminder = perceived ongoing value | Etsy printable poster sets sell $12–20 |
| 6 | **The Grocery Field Mission Pack** — 6 store missions with parent answer keys | **$17** | *"My kid won't sit and do a workbook."* Converts the product from a desk activity into an outing — and drives your best TikTok content | Same Etsy activity-pack band |
| 7 | **The Print Perfect Pack** — grayscale-optimized versions of every page, dyslexia-friendly type variant, and a one-page "how to print this so it doesn't look bad" guide | **$12** | *"My printer is black-and-white / out of ink / this'll look like garbage."* A real, unaddressed objection in an all-digital category (market-research §A.3) | Modest by design. Its job is credibility, not stated value |
| 8 | **The Label Change Briefing** — what the FDA's proposed front-of-pack "Nutrition Info Box" (saturated fat / sodium / added sugars, rated Low-Med-High) will change, and free lifetime updates when it lands | **$19** | *"Will this be out of date?"* FDA projected a final rule in spring 2026, with compliance 3–4 years after the effective date ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/front-package-nutrition-labeling) · [Food Engineering](https://www.foodengineeringmag.com/articles/103400-fda-could-finalize-front-of-pack-nutrition-label-rule-in-spring-2026)) | "Lifetime updates" is a real, ongoing obligation. **No competitor in Section A addresses this at all** |

### The math

| Line | Value |
|---|---|
| The Parent Guide | $27 |
| Kids Workbook, Ages 6–9 | $19 |
| Kids Workbook, Ages 10–13 | $19 |
| Label Showdown Game Pack (Tier 1) | $17 |
| **Core subtotal** | **$82** |
| Bonuses 1–8 | $184 |
| **TOTAL VALUE** | **$266** |
| **Today** | **$47** *(launch $37)* |
| Ratio | **5.7x** |

**Why this is defensible and most bonus stacks aren't:** every line is priced against a comparable that actually transacts — Big Life Journal at $24–29 for one book, Etsy printables at $12–20, TPT site licenses at ~$89. The core books are valued *below* a single Big Life Journal despite being longer. If a buyer audits this, it holds.

**Do not:** value the Parent Guide at $97 because it's "worth it." Big Life Journal gives theirs away free. Inflated stacks are the #1 reason a premium parenting product gets called a scam in the comments — and per Risk 9 below, comment-section credibility is the whole asset.

- Tier 0 card finished and live on a Payhip/Stan landing page

- ✅ Educational-use-only + not-medical-advice disclaimer on every PDF and the sales page

|---|---|---|---|

## I. REVENUE MODEL

**Read this first:** these are **month-12 run rates**, not month-1. The model is built on the funnel chain in market-research §E, where **~100,000 views ≈ 2 direct sales**. Direct-from-video revenue is small in every scenario; **the list is where the money is** in all three. That is the finding, not an artifact of the assumptions.

### Assumptions table

| Assumption | Conservative | Realistic | Aggressive | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly video views | 300,000 | 1,000,000 | 4,000,000 | [ESTIMATE] — follower count unknown; modeled on views, which is the honest driver |
| Posting cadence | 1/day | 1–2/day | 2/day + 1 viral/mo | — |
| View → profile visit | 1.5% | 1.8% | 2.2% | [ESTIMATE] — no reliable public benchmark found. **Weakest number in the model** |
| Profile visit → bio click | 5.0% | 5.5% | 6.5% | Benchmark 3–8%, >10% excellent ([SocialRails](https://socialrails.com/blog/how-to-add-link-tiktok-bio-complete-guide)); a more conservative source says 1–5% |
| Bio click → direct purchase | 2.0% | 2.2% | 2.5% | Below the quoted 3.4% link-in-bio average ([Crescitaly](https://blog.crescitaly.com/tiktok-link-in-bio-strategy-profile-visits-sales-2026/)) because that is a **vendor claim** on warmer traffic |
| Bio click → email opt-in | 25% | 25% | 28% | [ESTIMATE] — anchored to waitlist median ~11% of page visitors ([LaunchList](https://getlaunchlist.com/tools/waitlist-benchmark)), adjusted up for a strong free offer to already-interested clickers |
| Email list → monthly purchase | 2.0% | 2.5% | 2.5% | Ecommerce promo email converts 1.5–3% ([Athenic](https://getathenic.com/blog/email-marketing-conversion-rate-guide)) |

| Platform + processing fees | 8% | 8% | 8% | Payhip free (5%) + Stripe (2.9% + $0.30) |

### Month-12 math — shown so it can be challenged

**CONSERVATIVE**
```
300,000 views × 1.5%              =   4,500 profile visits
4,500 × 5.0%                      =     225 bio clicks
225 × 2.0%                        =     4.5 direct sales × $31   =   $140/mo
225 × 25%                         =      56 new subs/mo
List @ month 12 (56×12, 3% churn) ≈     560 subscribers
560 × 2.0% × $39                  =    11.2 sales             =   $437/mo
                                       GROSS  =  $577/mo
                        less refunds 4%       = –$23
                        less fees 8%          = –$44
                                       NET    ≈  $510/mo   →  ~$6,100/yr
```

**REALISTIC**
```
1,000,000 views × 1.8%            =  18,000 profile visits
18,000 × 5.5%                     =     990 bio clicks
990 × 2.2%                        =    21.8 direct sales × $31  =   $675/mo
990 × 25%                         =     248 new subs/mo
List @ month 12 (3% churn)        ≈   2,480 subscribers
2,480 × 2.5% × $39                =    62 sales               = $2,418/mo
                                       GROSS  = $3,093/mo
                        less refunds 5%       = –$155
                        less fees 8%          = –$247
                                       NET    ≈ $2,691/mo  →  ~$32,300/yr
```

**AGGRESSIVE**
```
4,000,000 views × 2.2%            =  88,000 profile visits
88,000 × 6.5%                     =   5,720 bio clicks
5,720 × 2.5%                      =     143 direct sales × $33  = $4,719/mo
5,720 × 28%                       =   1,602 new subs/mo
List @ month 12 (4% churn)        ≈  15,400 subscribers
15,400 × 2.5% × $41               =   385 sales              = $15,785/mo
                                       GROSS  = $20,504/mo
                        less refunds 6%       = –$1,230
                        less fees 8%          = –$1,640
                                       NET    ≈ $17,634/mo →  ~$211,600/yr
```

### Summary

| | Conservative | Realistic | Aggressive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly views | 300k | 1M | 4M |
| Month-12 list size | ~560 | ~2,480 | ~15,400 |
| Sales/mo (month 12) | ~16 | ~84 | ~528 |
| **Net revenue/mo (month 12)** | **~$510** | **~$2,691** | **~$17,634** |
| **Annualized run rate** | **~$6.1k** | **~$32.3k** | **~$212k** |
| Share of revenue from **email** | **76%** | **78%** | **77%** |

### ⚠️ Challenge these three numbers before you trust any of it

1. **The 1.5–2.2% view→profile-visit rate is the load-bearing assumption and I could not find a credible public benchmark for it.** Every dollar above flows through it. If your real rate is 0.5%, divide all three scenarios by three. **You can measure this in your own TikTok analytics in five minutes — do that before planning around any of these figures.**
2. **The aggressive case assumes a 15,400-person list still converts at 2.5%/month in month 12.** It won't. Your buyers are removed from the buyable pool, and label literacy is a *one-time purchase*. Expect list conversion to decay toward 1–1.5% by month 12 unless you ship new products. **Realistically, haircut the aggressive case by 30–40% → ~$130–150k.** This is exactly the problem a Tier 4 / second product solves, and it is the strongest argument for the card deck.
3. **Refund rates of 4–6% are above the healthy 1–2% band.** I modeled them high on purpose because TikTok impulse buyers refund hard. **If you actually hit 6%, something in the offer is over-promising** — fix the sales page, don't accept it as a cost of doing business.

**And the honest headline: the conservative case is a side income, not a job.** 300k views/month producing ~$510/month is the realistic outcome for most creators who execute this well. Plan for that. The realistic case only arrives with 1M views/month sustained — roughly a year of daily posting.

## J. RISK REGISTER

| # | Risk | Severity | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **TikTok Shop enforcement / account ban** for listing digital goods as physical, or a QR-card workaround. Explicitly flagged: *"uploading an ebook as a physical product to bypass this is a guaranteed ban"* ([Minea](https://www.minea.com/dropshipping-winning-products/best-niche-products-dropshipping/can-you-sell-digital-products-on-tiktok-shop)) | 🔴 **Catastrophic** — takes your affiliate income with it | Medium *if tempted* | **Do not sell digital on TikTok Shop, in any disguise.** Bio link only. If you ever do physical, ship a genuinely substantive product |
| 2 | **Platform dependency** — TikTok is a rented audience with a live history of US ban efforts ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efforts_to_ban_TikTok_in_the_United_States)); TikTok Shop provides no mechanism to build an email list ([EasyApps](https://easyappsecom.com/guides/shopify-vs-tiktok-shop-2026)) | 🔴 High | Medium | **The email list is the mitigation.** Every video's job is a subscriber, not a sale. Mirror all content to Reels + Shorts from day 1 — same file, zero extra cost |
| 3 | **FTC health-claims exposure.** The FTC substantiation standard is *rigorous* for health claims; vague qualifiers like "may" or "helps" are inadequate; **a disclaimer does not cure an otherwise deceptive ad** ([FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)) | 🔴 High | Low–Medium | Sell **a reading skill**, never a health outcome. Banned from all copy: "healthier kids," "prevent obesity/diabetes," "avoid toxic ingredients." Allowed: "read a label in 10 seconds," "understand serving sizes." A `09-QA/` copy red-team pass on every sales asset before publish |

## WHERE I'D PUSH BACK

**1. The build order in the brief is backwards, and this is my strongest objection.** The directory contract implies building the parent guide, both kid tiers, printables, video scripts and the build pipeline before anything ships. That is 200+ hours against a category whose verified digital floor is **$1.75**, whose Etsy printable ceiling is **~$20**, and whose premium physical ceiling is **~$29**. **Ship Tier 1 ($17 game pack) in two weeks. Sell it. Then build.** If the game pack doesn't sell at $17, the $47 system won't sell either — and you'll have learned that for two weeks of work instead of three months.

**2. $47 is right for Tier 2, but be ready for it to be wrong.** It's anchored — 3 books vs Big Life Journal's ~$25–29 for one, sitting at the floor of the $47–97 premium band. But **every single verified competitor selling *digital* in this exact topic is under $20.** You are asking for a 2.5x premium over the entire observed digital market on the strength of design quality the buyer can't fully assess before purchase. **Run a $37 vs $47 split in week 2.** If $37 converts more than 1.35x better, the market has told you its price and you should listen.

**3. Tier 3 at $97 is the weakest tier in the ladder.** The classroom-license buyer and the medically-obligated-family buyer are *completely different people* and I've bundled them because both will pay $97 — which is a pricing convenience, not a customer insight. It will work as an anchor regardless. But if Tier 3 sells at all well, **split it**: a Classroom/Co-op License and a separate Medical Household Edition, each priced on its own logic. Don't sell a school a carb-counting module or a diabetic family a lesson rubric.

**4. The bonus stack is honest, and that costs you conversion — take the trade knowingly.** A $266 → $47 offer (5.7x) converts worse than the $1,200 → $47 stacks you'll see all over TikTok. I've kept every value anchored to a real transacting comparable because your buyer is a homeschool mom who prices curriculum for a living and a medical parent who has been burned by wellness grifters. **In this specific market, credibility outsells hype.** But be clear-eyed: you are choosing a lower conversion rate for a lower refund rate, better reviews, and a brand that survives. I think that's correct. It is still a trade.

**5. The revenue model's most important finding is the one nobody wants: ~76–78% of revenue comes from email in every scenario.** If you take one thing from this document, it isn't the pricing. It's that **the TikTok content's job is to build an email list, not to sell a product.** A creator who posts daily and sends no email will hit the conservative case at best, no matter how good the product is.

**6. I'd challenge the "premium digital product" framing entirely, one more time.** Verified facts: the entire $24–29 tier in this category is *physical*. The entire $1.75–$20 tier is *digital*. Format sets the ceiling, not quality. Digital is unquestionably the right *first* move — zero COGS, instant delivery, no inventory, no 1–31 day TikTok payout lag. But the highest-value long-term asset here is probably the **Label Showdown card deck** at ~$5–7/unit ([QinPrinting](https://www.qinprinting.com/custom-card-deck-cost/)) sold at $29–34: un-pirateable, giftable to the grandparent persona, legitimately sellable on TikTok Shop, and the only version of this product that demos in 15 seconds on camera. **Fund it from digital profits in month 3–4. Don't start there — the MOQ is 500 units / $2,500–3,500 cash — but don't lose sight of it either.** That deck is the version of this business with a real moat.
