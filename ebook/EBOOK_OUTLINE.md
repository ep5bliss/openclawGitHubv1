## PART 1: FOR BUYERS

### Chapter 1 — The Closing Cost Trap
**The secret:** Your pre-approval letter shows your monthly payment. It does not show the $15,000–$35,000 you'll need at the closing table.
- Full breakdown: loan origination (0.5–1%), title insurance (0.5–1%), appraisal ($300–600), home inspection ($400–600), attorney fees, recording fees, prepaid taxes/insurance into escrow
- The 2–5% rule and what it means in dollar terms on a $300k, $400k, $500k home
- What to ask for: "Give me a Loan Estimate within 3 days of application" (legally required)
- Negotiating seller concessions to cover closing costs
- **Checklist:** Full closing cost line-item worksheet

### Chapter 2 — The Inspection Game
**The secret:** Agents in competitive markets will pressure you to waive the inspection. This can cost you $20,000–$100,000+.
- What inspectors find vs. what they miss
- The difference between a general inspection, pest inspection, sewer scope, radon test, mold test
- How to make an offer with an inspection contingency that still looks competitive
- Red flags inspectors found that agents downplayed
- How to negotiate repairs vs. price reductions after inspection
- **Checklist:** Pre-inspection and post-inspection action items

### Chapter 3 — Dual Agency: The Agent Who Works for Both Sides
**The secret:** In most states, your buyer's agent can also represent the seller. They get both commissions. Their job is now to close the deal, not protect you.
- What dual agency is and how to spot it
- States where it's illegal vs. states where it's allowed
- How to ask: "Are you the listing agent or do you know the listing agent?"
- Why "designated agency" is barely better
- What to do if your agent suggests it
- **Script:** How to decline dual agency politely but firmly

### Chapter 4 — The Fake Urgency Playbook
**The secret:** "There are three other offers" is one of the most common lies in real estate. You have the right to ask for proof.
- How fake competing offer situations work
- The "highest and best" tactic — what it means and when it's real vs. manufactured
- Escalation clauses: when they help and when they're used against you
- Your legal rights: asking for proof of other offers
- How to think about offering strategy without getting emotionally hijacked
- The right question to ask: "What does the seller actually need?"

### Chapter 5 — HOA Hell: The Monthly Bill Nobody Mentioned
**The secret:** Nearly half of all homes have an HOA. Most buyers don't know they can inherit unpaid assessments.
- HOA fee ranges ($100–$1,000+/month)
- Special assessments: the one-time bills that can hit $5,000–$50,000
- How to get HOA financials BEFORE you're under contract
- What to look for: reserve fund levels, pending litigation, deferred maintenance
- Rules that affect daily life (parking, rentals, pets, exterior changes)
- **Checklist:** 12 questions to ask every HOA before you buy

### Chapter 6 — Mortgage Rate Secrets
**The secret:** Your lender may not volunteer that you can buy your rate down. Your agent may not mention that the seller can pay for it.
- Mortgage rate buydowns (temporary and permanent) explained in plain English
- Points: when paying upfront makes sense
- Seller-paid closing costs and rate buydowns as negotiating tools
- Adjustable vs. fixed — what the numbers actually mean for your budget
- Why you should get 3 loan quotes (most buyers get 1)
- **Script:** How to ask a lender for a rate buydown quote

### Chapter 7 — Your New Rights: The Post-NAR Buyer's Agreement
**The secret:** As of August 2024, you must sign a buyer's agent agreement before touring homes. Most agents present this as routine paperwork. It isn't — it's a negotiable contract.
- What the buyer's agent agreement says (translated from legalese)
- What's negotiable: commission rate, duration, exclusivity, property type
- The average buyer's agent commission (2.34–2.55% in 2025) — and what lower alternatives look like
- Rebate agents, flat-fee agents, hourly agents: real options explained
- How to interview an agent before signing
- **Script:** How to negotiate your buyer's agent agreement

### Chapter 8 — The First-Year Budget Nobody Gave You
**The secret:** Your mortgage payment is just the beginning. 56% of first-time buyers set aside nothing for repairs.
- The 1–3% annual maintenance rule and what it means monthly
- Property tax escrow adjustments (why your payment can jump in Year 2)
- Homeowner's insurance cost increases
- The major systems and their lifespans (HVAC, roof, water heater, appliances)
- How to build a home emergency fund
- **Worksheet:** First-year true cost of homeownership calculator

### Chapter 9 — How to Choose an Agent Who Actually Works for You
- The difference between listing agents and buyer's agents
- How to find agents with track records (not just good reviews)
- Questions to ask in the interview
- Red flags to walk away from
- The flat-fee and discount agent landscape (Redfin, Houwzer, local flat-fee brokers)
- **Script:** 10-question agent interview guide

### Chapter 10 — Buyer's Master Scripts and Checklists
- Pre-search checklist
- Making an offer checklist

## PART 2: FOR SELLERS

### Chapter 11 — Commission Is Always Negotiable
**The secret:** The "standard" 5–6% commission is not a law. It's a tradition. And since August 2024, even the buyer's agent portion is now in your hands.
- How commissions actually work (and who gets what)
- The NAR settlement: what changed, what didn't, and what it means for your check at closing
- How to negotiate: "What's your best rate if I list with you?" — and what to expect
- Flat-fee MLS services: what they are and when they make sense
- **Script:** How to negotiate your listing agent's commission

### Chapter 12 — Pocket Listings: When Your Agent Sells You Short
**The secret:** A pocket listing is a home sold before it ever hits the MLS. Great for the agent's network. Often terrible for your final sale price.
- What a pocket listing is and how it works
- Why agents do it: double commissions, investor relationships, quick closes
- The data: MLS-listed homes sell for 5–13% more on average than off-market sales
- Zillow's 2025 ban on pocket listings — what changed
- How to ensure maximum exposure for your property
- **Clause:** Language to add to your listing agreement requiring MLS listing

### Chapter 13 — Pricing Strategy: Are They Underpricing You?

- How agents determine list price (and how to verify their math)
- The anchoring trap: listing low to create a bidding war vs. listing right
- How to read a Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) yourself
- Getting an independent appraisal before listing
- When an overpriced listing is YOUR mistake vs. agent's mismanagement
- **Worksheet:** DIY price validation using Zillow/Redfin/public records

### Chapter 14 — Dual Agency for Sellers: Just Say No

### CONCLUSION: 10 Rules Every Buyer and Seller Should Live By (2–3 pages)
1. Never waive your inspection without fully understanding the risk
2. Always get a Loan Estimate in writing within 3 days
3. Get three loan quotes, not one
4. Interview at least three agents before signing anything
5. Read every contract before you sign it (especially buyer's agent agreements)
6. Commission is always negotiable — always
7. Maximum MLS exposure is non-negotiable for sellers
8. The highest offer is not always the best offer
9. Build 1–3% of home value as a maintenance reserve every year
10. Trust but verify — ask for documentation, not just assurances
